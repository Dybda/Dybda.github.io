Content, Not Selection: Course Exposure, Political Orientation, and Civic Engagement in College

Separating course content from selection in the political effects of college

Status: Under review Methods: Panel & administrative data / General-education requirement / LLM ensemble

Abstract

Does college shape students' politics, or do politically distinctive students simply choose similar courses? Research on fields of study cannot easily tell the two apart: a major is chosen in advance, bundling what a curriculum teaches with the peers and self-selection that come with it. I separate content from selection by measuring exposure not by field but by course content, scoring the communicative and cultural content of 6,635 course descriptions with an ensemble of large language models, and by exploiting a general-education requirement that compels coursework students cannot sort on.

Using panel and administrative data from a large public university, I find that this content substantially raises civic engagement (0.3 SD) while shifting political orientations only modestly leftward (0.11 SD). Selection works in opposite directions on the two outcomes, masking the orientation effect and inflating the civic improvement. Coursework cultivates engaged citizens more than partisans; fears of ideological indoctrination in the curriculum are largely misplaced while the civic gains are larger than prior research has been able to demonstrate.

Key Contributions

  • Measures exposure by course content rather than by field or degree completion
  • Uses a general-education requirement to identify content effects students cannot sort on
  • Shows selection biases the two outcomes in opposite directions — masking the orientation effect, inflating the civic one
  • LLM-ensemble scoring of 6,635 course descriptions for communicative and cultural content

Data & Methods

  • Panel and administrative data from a large public university
  • General-education requirement as a source of non-sortable exposure
  • Ensemble of large language models scoring course-description content
  • Panel estimation separating content effects from selection into coursework
